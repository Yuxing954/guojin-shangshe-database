L24: # Gold ETF Flows: October 2026
L25: 
L26: ## Europe remains in driver's seat
L29: Published: 5 November, 2026
L30: ### Highlights
L31: 
L32:   * Global gold ETFs continued to expand, adding US$2.7bn in October, led by European funds.
L33:   * Global gold ETF holdings rose 19t to 4,275t, but their AUM fell 6% to US$542bn due to the lower gold price in the month.
L34:   * Global gold market trading volumes pulled back, due mainly to cooling activity in exchange-traded derivatives.
L35: ### October in review
L36: 
L37: Global physically-backed gold ETFs【7†^{1}】 attracted US$2.7bn in October, having expanded for four consecutive months (Chart 1). Notably, European funds led the charge with sizable inflows. Asian investors remained net buyers while North America saw marginal outflows. Global gold ETFs’ collective holdings climbed 19t to 4,275t, another month-end record. Meanwhile, assets under management (AUM) fell 6% to US$542bn as the lower gold price overshadowed inflows.
L38: During the first ten months of the year, global gold ETFs added US$41bn, the third strongest on record, and their holdings rose by 246t. European and Asian investors led global flows, each accounting for 44% and 45% of total net inflows, respectively. At the country level, the UK recorded the largest inflows, with China a close second.

L47: ### Regional overview
L48: 
L49: North American funds saw modest outflows of US$512mn, the only region with outflows in October. US-domiciled gold ETFs were the sole driver of the region’s October loss; Canadian funds continued to attract inflows.
L50: US technology equities came under selling pressure during October amid growing concerns around AI-related capital spend. The resulting market stress may have contributed to the region’s gold ETF outflows as some investors turned to their gold holdings as a ready source of liquidity. This illustrates one of gold’s key attributes: deep liquidity can help investors meet cash needs during market turbulence.
L60: Japanese gold ETFs continued to attract inflows in October, driven by similar factors: the Nikkei pulled back and Japanese government bond yields fell amid safe-haven flows and less aggressive expectations of the BoJ’s tightening ahead for most of the month – the central bank left rates unchanged in late October.【12†^{5}】 India also saw sustained gold ETF buying in the month despite local mutual funds’ 【13†temporary suspension of fresh subscription to gold fund of funds】 in mid-October.
L61: Gold ETFs listed in other regions saw mild inflows of US$62mn, supported throughout the month by demand from both Australia and South Africa.

L90: ### Volumes eased
L91: 【20†Global gold market volumes】【21†^{6}】 declined 13% in October, averaging US$365bn per day. All sectors saw volumes pull back. Over-the-counter (OTC) volumes decreased by a marginal 2%, to US$219bn/day – LBMA activities remained healthy at US$196bn/day, with only a 2% m/m decline and well above the 2025 average of US$161bn/day. Exchange-traded activity fell the most, shedding 25% in the month to US$140bn/day, led by a 22% decline at the COMEX and a 31% retreat in Shanghai.
L92: Liquidity of global gold ETFs averaged US$6bn/day in the month, a 20% m/m fall, primarily driven by North America which accounted for the lion’s share of total.
L93: 【22†Total COMEX net longs】 fell further, shedding 94t in October to 560t, the lowest since end-July.【23†^{7}】 Net longs held by managed money reduced 53t in the month. Meanwhile, other reportables【24†^{8}】 continued to retreat, losing 54t, and spreads【25†^{9}】 pulled back by 13t.
L94: Interestingly, net open interest【26†^{10}】 of COMEX gold options rose 6% in the month, and kept climbing during the last few days of October despite the equity sell-off, possibly due to rollovers amid contract expiry and potential dip buying.

L103: ## Gold ETFs holdings and flows
L104: 
L105: *We monitor how fund assets change through time by looking at two key metrics: demand and fund flows.
L106: 
L107:   * Gold ETF demand is the change in gold holdings during a given period. We use this metric to calculate the quarterly demand estimates reported in Gold Demand Trends.
L108: 
L109:   * Fund flows represent the amount of money – reported in US dollars – that investors have put into (or retrieved from) a fund during a given period. For more details, see our 【17†methodology note】.
L110: † 'Global Inflows/positive demand’ refers to the sum of changes of all funds that saw a net increase in holdings over a given period (e.g., month, quarter, etc.). Conversely, ‘global outflows/negative demand’ aggregates changes from funds that saw holdings decline over the same period.

L27: ### Regional and fund-specific analysis of gold holdings and flows in USD.
L28: Gold-backed ETFs and similar products account for a significant part of the gold market, with institutional and individual investors using them to implement many of their investment strategies. Flows in ETFs often highlight short-term and long-term opinions and desires to holding gold. The data on this page tracks gold held in physical form by open-ended ETFs and other products such as close-end funds, and mutual funds. Most funds included in this list are fully backed by physical gold.

L51: Equity sell-offs in the month also led to strong safe-haven buying of US Treasuries. October saw their strongest monthly return since December 2023 and pulled yields down from the highest in nearly 25 years.【9†^{2}】 Relatively attractive price levels and investor repricing of a less aggressive US Fed may have increased their appeal. This focus on US Treasuries could have diverted investors away from gold.
L52: Yet it is worth noting that US gold ETFs saw net inflows, albeit mild, during the last week of October when local equities plunged again. This reflects investors’ strategic allocation, possibly driven by a combination of diversification needs against political uncertainty – with midterm elections around the corner – and persisting fiscal concerns.
